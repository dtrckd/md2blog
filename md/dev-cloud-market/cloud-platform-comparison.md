## Final analysis — European cloud providers, state as of now

### Verdict on your original three
All three are still valid, independent, and correctly named. But you were missing the single biggest one (**Hetzner**) and the whole German sovereign-money tier (**StackIT**, **IONOS**).

### What changed since common knowledge
- **Platform.sh no longer exists under that name** — rebranded to **Upsun** (Sept 2025). Same team/tech.
- **Nebius is not a European sovereign cloud** — it's the ex-Yandex international spin-off, Nasdaq-listed (NBIS), ~$55bn cap, pure AI/GPU infra. Drop it from a sovereignty shortlist.
- **Gcore** took its first external capital in 2024 ($60M Series A led by Wargaming, w/ Constructor Capital — Serg Bell). Luxembourg HQ, but the ownership lineage is a sovereignty flag.
- **Infomaniak** transferred majority voting rights to a public-interest **Foundation** (permanent, un-acquirable) and is preparing a **SIX listing** via reverse takeover of Perrot Duval.
- **OVHcloud**: Octave Klaba returned as CEO (Oct 2025) after weak FY26 guidance (5–7% vs. ~10% expected).
- **Scaleway**: datacenters spun into **OpCore** (€860m EV, 50% InfraVia), Dec 2024.
- **UpCloud**: no acquisition. Still independent, Tesi/Inventure-backed.

### The two real axes

**1. Balance-sheet sovereignty** — StackIT (Lidl money), IONOS (listed, United Internet 63.8%), Aruba (PE), T-Systems (DT), Exoscale (A1 Telekom). They out-fund the pure-plays and win the compliance paperwork.

**2. Independent purists** — Hetzner (100% founder-owned, never took a euro), Infomaniak (foundation-locked), UpCloud (VC but small). Trade scale for trust.

OVH and Scaleway straddle both, and are the only two with real hyperscaler-shaped ambition.
